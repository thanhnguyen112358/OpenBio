# Q2 · Does the placental expression footprint recur across pregnancies?

**Working hypothesis.** EOPE placentas may share an angiogenic RNA signal at delivery while varying substantially in glycolytic stress. Ask whether those programs rise in the same pregnancies and how much their variation tracks fetal growth restriction (FGR) or delivery age. Ackerman found a metabolic pattern in a subset of this cohort. Leavey’s larger study gives independent context for heterogeneous placental expression. The 15 EOPE cases here support describing continuous variation and identifying patterns for follow-up. [Ackerman et al.](https://pmc.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/articles/PMC10192030/) · [Leavey et al.](https://pubmed.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/27160201/)

## Cohort and analysis

1. Use one GSE203507 villous sample per pregnancy. Plot age and QC across all 30 donors, then repeat PCA in the 25 preterm donors. Keep the source group labels. Use decimal `ga_delivery_weeks` and inspect whether age, FGR, fetal sex, or an individual donor explains the main pattern. Six of 15 EOPE cases overlap the `iPTB` delivery-age range, and only one of those six has FGR. [Villous metadata](openbio-datathon-2026/track-01/data/track01_metadata_vt.csv) · [Data dictionary](openbio-datathon-2026/track-01/data/track01_data_dictionary.md)
2. Fit a four-level preterm `group` model on raw counts with `~ ga_delivery_weeks + group`. Show the unadjusted fit and use fetal sex as a separate sensitivity check. Compare `EOPE − iPTB`, `(EOPE+FGR) − FGR`, `FGR − iPTB`, and `(EOPE+FGR) − EOPE`. For the prespecified panel, compare the two PE effects directly using `[(EOPE+FGR) − FGR] − [EOPE − iPTB]`. Sparse age overlap makes that interaction exploratory. Retain term births as descriptive context.
3. Predefine `FLT1`, `PGF`, `LDHA/LDHB`, `PDK1/GOT1`, and one fixed hypoxia or glycolysis gene set. Calculate the ratios as differences of log-normalized expression using a documented offset. Plot the four ratio components too so a falling denominator is visible. Keep `FLT1` and `PGF` as separate RNA measures. The metabolic ratios benchmark the source study and provide a proxy for an expression program.
4. Put individual pregnancies at the center. Plot the two metabolic ratios against each other and align donor-level `FLT1` and `PGF` panels to the same donor IDs. Color by clinical group and show scores against delivery age. Check age-restricted subsets where supported and leave out one donor at a time to identify influential placentas. Use continuous scores throughout.
5. Log at least three Zeisel browser lookups and compare the signals with cell-identity markers. If cell localization becomes the main question, use Admati syncytiotrophoblast pseudobulk as an exploratory donor-level analysis. If recurrence is the main question, carry the same prespecified scores into GSE114691 after checking its phenotype and tissue definitions. [Organizer workflow](openbio-datathon-2026/track-01/3-workflow.md) · [Browser guide](openbio-datathon-2026/track-01/data/interpretation/zeisel_browser_guide.md) · [GSE114691](https://www.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/geo/query/acc.cgi?acc=GSE114691)

## Figures and follow-up

Prepare three figures for delivery-age overlap and PCA, the four preterm contrasts with intervals, and the paired angiogenic and metabolic profiles. Recurring angiogenic shifts with widely varying metabolic scores would support two partly independent features of this cohort. A coherent rise in both programs across most cases would favor a shared program of varying magnitude. If the pattern follows age or hinges on one donor, show the sensitivity directly. An independent cohort with blinded pathology could test whether a high metabolic score tracks maternal vascular malperfusion among pregnancies with similar delivery age and FGR status.
