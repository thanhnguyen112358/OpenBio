*The literature supports altered glycolytic regulation in pre-eclampsia (PE), but not a uniform “OXPHOS-to-glycolysis switch” across the placenta.* The strongest current interpretation is *cell-type-, developmental-stage-, and disease-subtype-specific metabolic dysfunction*: excessive glycolysis can accompany inflammatory injury, while inadequate glycolysis can impair trophoblast proliferation, decidualization, and vascular repair. [pubmed.ncbi.nlm.nih](https://pubmed.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/?cmd=search&term=Aye+IL[au]&dispmax=50)

Scope: literature available through November 15, 2026, emphasizing placental and maternal–fetal-interface biology.

## The central distinction

A “glycolytic shift” can mean increased glycolytic-gene expression, increased glucose-to-lactate flux, or greater *relative dependence* on glycolysis because mitochondrial respiration has declined—these are not interchangeable measurements. Normal trophoblasts already use substantial aerobic glycolysis, and their metabolic phenotype changes with differentiation; increased lactate production is therefore not inherently pathological. [nature](https://www.nature.com/articles/srep42941)

The important question is consequently not simply *“Is glycolysis elevated in PE?”*, but **“In which cells, at what gestational stage, relative to which control, and with what functional consequence?”**—an interpretation supported by the opposing findings below. [link.springer](http://link.springer.com/article/10.1186/s10020-025-01201-w)

## Evidence supporting increased glycolysis

### Hypoxia and HIF-1α signaling

The conventional model remains relevant: persistent placental oxygen stress promotes HIF-associated metabolic remodeling, increased glycolytic activity, and mitochondrial abnormalities. Vangrieken and colleagues demonstrated this experimentally in hypoxia-exposed placental explants and BeWo cells, including increased glycolytic-enzyme activity and lactate production. [pmc.ncbi.nlm.nih](https://pmc.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/articles/PMC7802931/pdf/pone.0245155.pdf)

Human evidence is strongest for *early-onset, particularly severe early-onset, PE*, rather than an identical phenotype across all PE cases. A 2026 study by Johnson-Gonzalez and colleagues linked sustained placental HIF-1α elevation with increased glycolytic proteins, altered energy sensing, and diminished antioxidant capacity; however, protein abundance should not be mistaken for direct metabolic-flux measurement. [research.wright](https://research.wright.edu/en/publications/severe-early-onset-preeclampsia-is-significantly-associated-with-/)

An influential [2023 integrative transcriptomic study](https://www.frontiersin.org/journals/molecular-biosciences/articles/10.3389/fmolb.2023.1248771/full), combining 372 placental samples, identified an EOPE-associated glycolytic-expression signature, whereas late-onset samples were more heterogeneous. Its principal contribution is *transcriptional evidence*, not demonstration that glucose-to-lactate flux necessarily increases in every affected cell.

### Inflammatory immunometabolism

More recent work makes the case more specifically in *decidual macrophages*, rather than treating the placenta as one metabolic compartment. Liu and colleagues’ 2026 study reported increased glycolytic activity and reduced oxidative phosphorylation in PE-associated macrophages, alongside inflammatory polarization and impaired trophoblast function in co-culture. [sciencedirect](https://www.sciencedirect.com/science/article/abs/pii/S0143400426003127)

This is an important advance because the study included Seahorse measurements and pathway perturbations, connecting metabolism to cellular function rather than relying exclusively on enrichment scores. Nevertheless, its mechanistic experiments used THP-1-derived macrophages under inflammatory stimulation, which limits extrapolation to the full diversity of decidual macrophages in vivo. [biomed](https://biomed.news/bims-imicid/2026-07-05)

## Evidence against a universal shift

Several studies instead associate PE with *insufficient glycolysis in cells responsible for placentation or vascular repair*. [sciencedirect](https://www.sciencedirect.com/science/article/pii/S0143400426000743)

- *Trophoblast proliferation:* Xu and colleagues found reduced HLA-F/PKM2 activity in PE-associated trophoblast biology; restoring PKM2 activity supported proliferation in experimental models. [link.springer](http://link.springer.com/article/10.1186/s10020-025-01201-w)
- *Decidualization:* Two 2026 studies implicated reduced PDK1 or PFKFB4 signaling in defective glycolytic adaptation of endometrial stromal cells. [yeasen](https://www.yeasen.com/h5/literature/detail/2065008724874407937)
- *Vascular repair:* A 2026 study identified deficient Piezo1–YAP–PFKFB3 signaling in cord-blood-derived endothelial progenitor cells associated with PE. [pmc.ncbi.nlm.nih](https://pmc.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/articles/PMC13540663/)

These findings are not necessarily contradictory: a placenta may contain inflammatory cells with excessive glycolytic activity alongside trophoblast or vascular populations with inadequate metabolic capacity. However, *that coexistence remains an integrative hypothesis*, not a fully demonstrated spatial and temporal map of PE metabolism. [sciencedirect](https://www.sciencedirect.com/science/article/pii/S0143400426000743)

There is also an older counterpoint worth retaining: Bloxam et al. reported reduced placental pyruvate and lactate in severe PE, without evidence of reduced adenine-nucleotide energy status. That result cautions against assuming either universal lactate accumulation or inevitable ATP depletion. [pubmed.ncbi.nlm.nih](https://pubmed.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/3605274/)

## Lactylation is an emerging focus

A major development in 2025–2026 is the treatment of lactate not just as an endpoint of glycolysis, but as a participant in *protein regulation and inflammatory signaling*. The PKM2 and BCAT2 studies implicate distinct nonhistone lactylation sites, while the endothelial HK2 study connects glycolysis, lactylation, and pyroptosis. [europepmc](https://europepmc.org/article/med/40908874)

The distinction matters experimentally: elevated lactylation does *not by itself establish increased glycolytic flux*, and an effect of exogenous lactate does not establish its endogenous cellular source. These papers therefore strengthen mechanistic plausibility without establishing a single lactate-driven pathway shared by all PE phenotypes. [ovid](https://www.ovid.com/jnls/hyper/fulltext/10.1161/hypertensionaha.125.25378~lactylation-of-bcat2-k377-contributes-to-the-progression-of)

## What remains unresolved

*Causality and timing are the main limitations.* Delivery-time placental abnormalities cannot, by themselves, determine whether metabolic changes initiated defective placentation or developed in response to established disease. [pubmed.ncbi.nlm.nih](https://pubmed.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/?cmd=search&term=Aye+IL[au]&dispmax=50)

For interpreting studies—or designing a computational analysis—the most consequential issues are:

- *Expression versus flux:* Treat glycolytic pathway scores as transcriptional states, not direct measurements of metabolic throughput. [pmc.ncbi.nlm.nih](https://pmc.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/articles/PMC13264119/figure/fig2/)
- *Cell composition:* Trophoblast differentiation alone substantially changes metabolism, making bulk placental comparisons difficult to interpret. [nature](https://www.nature.com/articles/srep42941)
- *Model dependence:* Jar, BeWo, HTR-8/SVneo, macrophages, and cord-derived progenitor cells interrogate different biological processes. [pmc.ncbi.nlm.nih](https://pmc.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/articles/PMC7802931/pdf/pone.0245155.pdf)
- *Direction of intervention:* Inhibiting glycolysis may suppress inflammatory injury while impairing trophoblast growth or vascular repair elsewhere. [europepmc](https://europepmc.org/article/med/40908874)

Accordingly, *“dysregulated metabolic plasticity” is a more defensible organizing framework than “PE is a Warburg-like disease.”* This closely follows Aye and colleagues’ proposal that severe disease may reflect failure to appropriately reprogram placental metabolism. [pubmed.ncbi.nlm.nih](https://pubmed.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/?cmd=search&term=Aye+IL[au]&dispmax=50)

## Where to start reading

For a conceptual foundation, start with *Aye et al., “Placental energy metabolism in health and disease—significance of development and implications for preeclampsia,” *AJOG (2022; online 2020)**. Pair it with *Gou and Zhang, “Glycolysis: A fork in the path of normal and pathological pregnancy,” *FASEB Journal (2023)** for the broader maternal–fetal-interface perspective. [pubmed.ncbi.nlm.nih](https://pubmed.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/37889786/)

For the current mechanistic frontier, the most informative combination is the *2026 HIF-1α study*, *2025 HLA-F/PKM2 study*, and *2026 macrophage study*: together they illustrate why tissue compartment and functional context determine whether a glycolytic change is adaptive, insufficient, or injurious.
